Vilo Play · landing page · v1

A calm shell,
with colour that belongs to the game.

One system for every game page: Secret Santa, Who’s Who, Secret Office Missions, Step Challenge and the games after them. The page frame stays quiet. Each game brings its own colour and its own illustration, and nothing else.

01

Principles

Calm shell, colour from the game

Base colours are ink, paper and white. A game colour appears only inside its own card, frame, drawer, hero and highlight. A page never mixes two game colours.

Clickable or flat. Never in between.

If it can be clicked, it has a 2 px black outline and a 4 px hard shadow, and it presses in. If it cannot be clicked, it is flat, with a hairline border, no shadow and no hover.

Real words only

Copy comes from Vilo’s own pages. Numbers in demos are examples and are never presented as results. Text inside illustrations is never real words.

Motion is quiet and replays

Things enter once when they come into view and play again when you scroll back. Hover and click always answer in 120 ms. Reduced motion turns it all off.

Emphasis in headings

Headings are not underlined or highlighted. Emphasis comes from weight alone: the first part is bold and the last words are regular, like “The whole season in one Slack DM.”

Hebrew

Vilo has no official Hebrew typeface. Our proposal is Rubik for headings and Heebo for body, to be confirmed against Vilo’s Hebrew site.

Do

  • Sentence case in headings.
  • Keep body lines near 65 characters.
  • Bold first part, regular last words.

Don’t

  • No marker, underline or colour behind headline words.
  • No italics for emphasis.
  • No second typeface in headings.

06

Illustration language

One hero object per game, drawn as clean outlined SVG. Ordinary objects, traced from a reference photo of the real thing, then redrawn by hand in code. It should feel like a sticker you could pick up.

Secret SantaChristmas tree and a pile of gifts. The gifts fall and settle.
Who’s WhoMagnifier over three implied text bars. Only the glass moves, drifting over the bars in a loop.
Secret Office MissionsEnvelope that opens, with a clue card inside. The card rises out, goes back, and the flap closes.
Step ChallengePair of sneakers. The two shoes step in turn.

Anatomy of a drawing

OutlineOne Snout Black line, about 4.5 px at the 1600 × 1100 canvas, round caps and joins. Small details such as folds and stitches are single strokes at about 3.6 px.
FillFlat colour, or a smooth two-stop gradient on the main object only: soft to fill, or fill to strong. Side faces and secondary parts are flat.
Depth
A copy of the main shape, shifted about 12 px down and right, in the game’s strong colour. Under white objects use Scrubs Blue Light.
Text is implied
Rounded bars in Scrubs Blue Light or Scrubs Blue. Never real words, logos or lettering copied from a reference.
PerspectiveBoxes and stacks are real 3D boxes seen through one camera with one vanishing point, so every box closes and rests on the one below.
PatternTwo or three patterned objects per scene at most, dots or stripes. Everything else is plain.

Palette in drawings

The page’s own game colours plus the four illustration base colours, and nothing else. No derived shades. Depth and side faces reuse a colour already in the set.

Game fillGame strongGame softTennis YellowScrubs Blue LightScrubs BlueClaw WhiteSnout Black

Tennis Yellow is for small accents only: bow tips, a pendant, a star, a lace tip, a dot on a question mark.

Canvas and delivery

  • Canvas 1600 × 1100 px, centred, even margins. On the page the background is transparent.
  • One clean SVG with editable shapes. Gradients stay as linear gradients so they open correctly in Illustrator.
  • Also keep a PNG and a transparent PNG of each drawing.
  • Grain is off. No texture noise, no speed lines, no scattered dots.

Choosing a subject

  • An everyday object people recognise at a glance, such as a magnifier, an envelope, sneakers or a tree.
  • Fewer elements beat a busy scene: three text bars, one card, one small question mark.
  • Keep it physically believable, with correct perspective and objects that rest on each other.
  • No people. The palette has no skin tone, and a portrait did not fit the object style.

On the game cards

The same drawings sit on the cards at the bottom of every page, in a 140 px slot. They never touch the title, and the three cards are the same height. On hover the drawing moves, and it keeps moving while the pointer stays on the card.

Who’s WhoA daily clue about a colleague
Secret Office MissionsSmall secret missions, one reveal
Step ChallengeOffice vs office, one live board
  • Card background is the game fill, radius 16 px, with a hard Snout Black shadow offset 12 px right and 12 px up. A Play button sits in a drawer under the card.
  • Title 30 px, one line of description at 15 px, centred.
  • Clicking a card opens that game’s page: the hero and the demo section change, and the page takes the game colour.

In the hero

  • Each hero drawing loops by itself. On the Who’s Who, Secret Office Missions and Step Challenge pages the whole drawing also floats 6 px up and down, slowly.
  • A white message bubble floats over the drawing and drifts a few pixels side to side. It shows the game’s channel and its first message, such as #step-challenge and “Step Challenge starts Monday 👟”. Its position is chosen for each drawing so it never hides the main shape: beside the lens on Who’s Who, to the right of the envelope on Secret Office Missions, under the shoes on Step Challenge. It leans left by 6° on most pages and right by 6° on Secret Office Missions.
  • Under reduced motion everything holds still.

Moving the drawings

  • Animate one part, not the whole drawing: the glass, the shoes, the envelope flap and card, the gifts.
  • Who’s Who: the magnifier drifts around over the text bars. Step Challenge: the two shoes step in turn. Secret Office Missions: the front flap opens, the card rises out of the envelope, then it slides back and the flap closes. Secret Santa: the gifts fall in and settle while the tree stays still.
  • Opening and closing use different timing: the envelope opens flap first, then card, and closes card first, then flap.
  • Cards and heroes use inline SVG so the parts can move. The Secret Santa hero is an image file with its animation inside.

Do

  • Trace a real photo, then simplify.
  • Keep every shape outlined the same weight.
  • Leave room around the drawing so it never touches text.

Don’t

  • No faked 3/4 views made by sweeping a side view. Draw it again.
  • No portraits or faces.
  • No logos, brand marks or copied lettering. The airmail envelope’s printed words and plane were dropped.
07

Motion

Light and quick, in the spirit of monday.com. Everything uses one set of durations.

120 msPress and release
200 msColour and UI changes
350 msOpen and close
450 msEnter
600 msReveal
  • Easing cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1) for enter and reveal, ease-out for press.
  • Sections reveal when they enter the screen and play again when you scroll back to them. Counters start from zero again.
  • Groups arrive one after the other, 0.18 s apart: setup cards, quotes.
  • The demo steps advance by themselves while visible and stop for good once someone picks a step.
  • Snow falls on the Secret Santa page only. It belongs to the page, not to the window, so it scrolls with the content. It has three layers of flakes in one size, a few per tile, falling at different speeds with a slight sway. The footer’s “Pause animations” button stops it.
  • Honour prefers-reduced-motion: no reveal, no float, no snow, no autoplay.

Responsive

One layout, three steps down. Grids drop columns, the hero stacks, and the demo becomes a plain list.

WidthWhat changes
Above 980 pxSetup cards, quotes and game cards in 3 columns. HR numbers in 4. Prices in 6. The demo shows the step list beside one message window.

  • The card drawers and the drawing motion on game cards open on hover only. On a touch screen there is no hover, so tapping a card opens that game’s page and the drawer is not seen. This is still to be decided.
  • Phone widths have not been checked on every page, and the hero message positions are set for desktop. Treat these rules as the intended behaviour.

11

Copy

  • Only Vilo’s own text. Headlines, descriptions, steps and message examples come from the Vilo game pages. Nothing is invented. If something is missing it is marked in square brackets, like [Copy from Vilo: off season].
  • Examples are examples. Names, numbers and dates in demos are illustrative, and the page footer says so. Examples stay neutral, with no holiday references unless the game is a holiday game.
  • Sentence case. Headlines in two parts: a bold first line and a regular second line.
  • Messages sound like a person at work: short, friendly, one emoji at most. The sender is Vilo, a colleague, or a channel name such as #step-challenge.
  • No real words inside drawings. Implied text bars only.

13

Adding a game

A new game is one record, one colour set and one drawing. Nothing else changes.

  1. Pick the three game colours: fill, strong and soft. Check that fill reads against Snout Black outlines.
  2. Choose one everyday object and draw it with this guide. Name it game_name.svg.
  3. Add the game’s hero copy, four demo steps and example messages, using only text from Vilo’s own game page.
  4. Add a card for it in the games grid, with its drawing and one hover animation.
